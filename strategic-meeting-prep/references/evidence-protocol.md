# Evidence protocol — source discipline for meeting intelligence

Read when information is fragmented, private, contested, recent, or central to a high-stakes decision.

## Search order and relevance

1. **Primary meeting record:** invitation / organizer updates, agenda, attachments, official pre-read.
2. **Commitment memory:** directly related previous meeting notes, decision logs, named action items, follow-up messages.
3. **Operating context:** current project plan, metrics, contract/policy/technical documentation, latest signed or approved version.
4. **External validation:** official authority, regulator, company release, technical standards, financial filing, professional source. Secondary reporting as context.

Stop searching when additional documents no longer change the primary ask, options, risks, or anticipated questions. Narrow retrieval to meeting topic, date range, known participants, and specific deliverables. Search title and full-text variants before deciding something is missing.

## Connector playbook (use only connected, authorized sources)

**Calendar:** locate matching event by title, known participants, and interval; prefer event ID over fuzzy matching; verify timezone, start/end, recurrence exception, location/conferencing, guests, attachments, last change. Do not assume attendance from invitation alone. When many matches, disambiguate rather than merge contexts.

**Email:** search within directly related threads; look for agreed/declined items, deadlines, changed terms, decision makers, promises. Treat unilateral proposals as *proposed*, not *agreed*. Do not scan unrelated mailboxes for personal background.

**Documents/Drive/Notion:** look for owner-approved decisions, latest version/date, comments marked unresolved, scope, KPIs, contract terms, dependencies. A meeting note or draft is not proof of approval. Preserve links and document titles when surfaced. If two versions disagree, describe the conflict and verify authority.

**Web/public sources:** prefer official publications for policies and current product specs, primary reporting for statements, and recent independent evidence for contested claims. Check publication time vs event time. Avoid stale biographies, obsolete titles, cached prices, unsupported forecasts and reputational claims from weak sources.

## Claim ledger

Internally track each critical claim with these fields:

| Claim | Evidence/source | As-of date | Status | Relevance | What would verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Specific assertion | Source title/link or user's quote | Date or unknown | Confirmed / Reported / Inference / Unknown | Impact on objective | Verification path |

**Confirmed:** directly supported by reliable source or explicit first-hand user statement; still note the source and date for changing facts. **Reported:** source says so, but status is not verified. **Inference:** reasoned conclusion; state basis. **Unknown:** no sufficient evidence.

No citations without a real, accessible source. Do not copy source text at length. Redact private identifiers, credentials, incidental personal details, and commercially confidential information from materials intended for external sharing. If a sourced detail could be high-impact and is stale, flag it prominently rather than silently guessing.

## Research questions that pay off

- What has already been decided, by whom, and what remains open?
- What changed since the previous conversation?
- What numbers/claims must survive scrutiny?
- Which document is authoritative, and what evidence is absent?
- What new fact would cause a different recommendation?
- What is the minimum required decision for the meeting to be productive?

## Source conflict handling

Write: "Document A (dated X) says __; document B (dated Y) says __. Treat __ as unresolved; verify with [owner] before committing." Never resolve conflicting statements solely by picking the more convenient one. If a human-authored user statement conflicts with source materials, describe both neutrally.
