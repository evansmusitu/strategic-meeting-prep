# Meeting archetype playbooks

Select one primary and, if needed, one secondary archetype. Add only the indicated modules to the core brief.

## Executive / board / steering committee

**Result:** a decision, escalation, prioritization, or explicit defer-with-criteria. Lead with recommendation and decision request. Include one-page option comparison (value, cost, risk, reversibility, dependencies), data confidence, decision rights, pre-read, and what would change the recommendation. Ask: "What threshold would allow you to approve this today?" Watch for buried trade-offs, weak assumptions, and ambiguous ownership.

## Sales discovery / partnership

**Result:** understood problem, qualification, next mutual commitment; not a premature pitch. Research public business context, stated objectives, current workflow, economics where sourced, stakeholders, procurement gate. Build discovery questions around pain, consequences, urgency, decision process, and success metrics. Prepare a crisp value hypothesis marked *hypothesis*, proof points with sources, mutual action plan, and an agreed next step. Avoid asserting ROI without validated inputs.

## Negotiation / procurement

**Result:** acceptable agreement or a defensible no-deal/next step. Use stakeholder/negotiation reference. Set objective, target, BATNA/TBD, walk-away boundary/TBD, issues ranking, trades, authority to commit, verification before signing, and fair concessions. Prepare a non-escalatory response to deadline pressure and a way to pause for approval.

## Job interview / scholarship / admissions

**Result:** demonstrate fit and test mutual expectations. Map criteria in the actual job/course description to **specific** evidence and quantified accomplishments only if user-supplied. Prepare a 45–90-second introduction, 3–5 STAR/CAR examples, likely technical/behavioral questions, two thoughtful interviewer questions, weak-area explanations without concealment, and a concise close. For students, translate projects, coursework, volunteering and self-study into evidence; never invent credentials or experience. Check details such as interview format, attire, technical assessment requirements and documents. Rehearse one question at a time.

## Technical / engineering / architecture / operations

**Result:** resolve a design trade-off, define an experiment, or reduce risk. Summarize requirements, architecture, boundary assumptions, alternatives, performance/reliability/security constraints, failure modes, acceptance tests, unresolved measurements and owner. Keep technical claims testable; cite versioned specifications and measured benchmarks where available. Ask what happens under failure and how to roll back.

## Team 1:1 / performance / difficult conversation

**Result:** shared understanding, coaching, feedback, or a defined behavior change. Separate observed facts from interpretations. Use specific examples; invite the other perspective; balance empathy and accountability. Prepare exploratory questions and a follow-up behavior/measurement. Do not label someone with a diagnosis or speculate about private life.

## Workshop / cross-functional facilitation

**Result:** convergence, prioritized input or decision record. Clarify who can decide, participants' prep, decision rule, time boxes, facilitation mechanics, options, dissent handling, accessibility and action capture. Plan when to diverge (gather options) and converge (score against explicit criteria). Record dissent and owner-approved decisions.

## Legal / regulatory / reputational / crisis-related

**Result:** accurate facts, appropriate escalation, lawful decision process. Audit sources, scope privilege/confidentiality, distinguish evidence from allegation, identify approvers, counsel/subject-matter review, no speculative assurances, and a written action trail. If urgent safety or legal exposure is at stake, defer substantive commitments until qualified people verify them. Avoid drafting deceptive messaging or advising concealment.

## Hybrid meetings

Choose the primary outcome (e.g., a vendor contract decision during a technical review), then load only the 1–2 highest-value modules. Never expand every playbook into a giant report.
