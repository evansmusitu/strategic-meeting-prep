# Meeting brief quality gate, adversarial review, and test cases

## Six-gate inspection

A brief is **ready** only if each applicable gate can be answered yes or is transparently marked as blocked:

1. **Outcome precision:** Is the primary ask observable, specific, authorized, and time-bound where feasible?
2. **Epistemic integrity:** Are important facts sourced/dated where available, with assumptions and conflicts visible?
3. **Decision intelligence:** Are decision rights, success criteria, realistic alternatives and costs or evidence gaps clear?
4. **Adversarial readiness:** Has the strongest objection—not a strawman—been answered or assigned a verification route?
5. **Usability:** Could the user speak the opening naturally and follow the agenda in the time available?
6. **Closeability:** Is there a path to close with an owner, action, due date and follow-up mechanism without inventing agreement?

For high-stakes, perform a second adversarial pass:
- What could make this advice materially wrong?
- Which three missing facts would most change the decision?
- What would a skeptical counterpart ask first?
- Where could the user accidentally exceed authority, reveal private data or make an irreversible promise?
- What if the meeting is shortened by half? Which question and ask survive?
- Is the fallback credible and safe? If not, replace with "pause and verify."

## Quality levels

- **Ready:** high-value facts validated, central ask authorized, dominant risk mitigated; still disclose remaining gaps.
- **Conditionally ready:** usable strategy but important facts/logistics await verification.
- **Exploratory only:** meeting purpose, authority, or evidence too uncertain to support commitments; recommend discovery and safe next steps.

Avoid numerical scores unless expressly requested. Never promise a meeting outcome.

## Pressure drills

**Flash / 60 seconds:** Deliver objective, opener, three facts or assumptions, top questions, ask, watch-out. No research avalanche.

**Fifteen minutes:** Prioritize a one-page brief, 30-second intro, objections, key evidence, logistics check.

**Deep preparation:** Include documentary source ledger, stakeholder matrix, 2–3 strategic alternatives, red-team, role-play and a follow-up plan.

## Failure check examples

| Scenario | Correct skill behavior |
|---|---|
| The meeting calendar time conflicts with an email | Flag discrepancy; identify current authority and verification step, don't pick silently |
| No attendee names given | Use roles, not invented identities |
| A colleague's priorities are unknown | Label concern as hypothesis based on role, not as a private motive |
| Interviewee lacks stated experience | Use documented academic/projects experience and admit the gap |
| Vendor offer price absent | Keep monetary terms TBD; don't invent a target or walk-away number |
| User wants meeting prep in two minutes | Provide Flash mode immediately without exhaustive retrieval |
| Legal commitment may be required | Surface signatory authority and qualified-review gate |
| Asked to share a brief automatically | Require explicit authorization, inspect recipients and confidentiality before doing so |
| Past decision is proposed but unsigned | Label proposed/pending, not approved |
| Calendar/Gmail/Drive disconnected | Work with supplied facts and clearly mark verification tasks |

## Critical failure conditions (v1.1)

Any of these blocks **Ready** irrespective of an attractive narrative: invented live information, fabricated reference, claim of prior approval based only on a proposal, unauthorized sending/sharing, concealed material conflict, unsupported external number or price, false claimed authority, or personal profiling unsupported by meeting-relevant evidence. For a sensitive recommendation, a known critical unresolved issue defaults to `Conditionally ready` or `Exploratory only`.

## Verifiable evidence chain

For each consequential claim, identify the actual source and freshness; for each decision, identify the authority; for each meeting action, identify confirmed versus proposed status. A high-quality response does not need every field if irrelevant, but the omitted fields must not silently imply verified status. A preparatory draft never counts as a performed external action.
