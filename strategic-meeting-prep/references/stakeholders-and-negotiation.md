# Stakeholder and negotiation intelligence

Read for complex rooms, power asymmetry, disagreement, partnership, sales, sensitive 1:1s, or negotiations.

## Decision map

| Role | Questions | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Decision owner | Who can say yes/no? What criteria or approvals matter? | Clarify decision standard and sign-off path |
| Recommender | Who evaluates technical/financial merits? | Equip with concise evidence and trade-offs |
| Implementer | Who bears operational cost and timelines? | Seek feasibility, resourcing and ownership |
| Risk/compliance | Who can stop an unsafe or noncompliant plan? | Address constraints early and document unknowns |
| Sponsor | Who is accountable for outcome? | Link ask to explicitly stated priorities |
| Affected party | Who lives with the consequences but lacks a veto? | Surface impacts and listen fairly |

Use a power-versus-interest map *only* from observed organizational roles and actions. For each named person, list: decision role (confirmed/hypothesis), prior stated position (source), likely concern (role-based hypothesis), one question to ask, one mistake to avoid. Don't stereotype by demographic background or diagnose motivations.

## Opposition and hard questions

Steelman the opposing view. For each objection, write:

1. **Concern:** the best charitable reading of their argument.
2. **Evidence:** what is known and unknown.
3. **Response:** fact → implication → option.
4. **Boundary:** what cannot safely be promised.
5. **Question:** what criterion or information would move the decision?

Useful delivery: "That's an important risk. What we know is __; what remains unverified is __. My proposal is __, with __ as the decision gate. Would that address your concern?"

## Negotiation module — use only when applicable

- Define interests versus positions. Determine whether parties are negotiating price, scope, timing, risk allocation, rights, or relationship.
- Request *user-owned* constraints; if unavailable, label BATNA, target, reservation point, and concession limits **TBD**.
- Construct a concession ladder using conditional exchanges ("If we can obtain X, we could consider Y") instead of unilateral giveaways.
- Compare deal vs no-deal on total value, risk, implementation, and reversibility. Do not fabricate offers, market rates, legal terms, or counterparty intentions.
- Identify the walk-away signal, verification requirement, signatory authority, and which commitments need counsel or leadership approval.
- A fair disagreement is not failure. Preserve relationships, document unresolved points, and suggest next-step criteria.

## Sensitive conversations

For performance, conflict, or difficult feedback: distinguish observations from interpretations, explain impact, ask for the other perspective, confirm understanding, and seek specific future behavior. Avoid personal attacks, deception, false urgency or pressure tactics. For legal/regulatory matters, do not tell the user to conceal, destroy, falsify or misrepresent records.

## Failure modes

- Mapping participants as enemies instead of decision partners.
- Optimizing a persuasive script while the user's actual authority is unclear.
- Confusing silence with agreement, invitation with attendance, or suggestion with approved action.
- Over-confident "read the room" claims unsupported by observed behavior.
- Negotiation without a realistic outside option, deadlines, sign-off authority or downside check.
